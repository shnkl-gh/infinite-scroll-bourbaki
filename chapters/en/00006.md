# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ satisfying the following axioms:

($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) *For every* $x \in E$, $(x \star x) \star (x \star x) = (x \star x) \star x$.

($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) *For all* $x, y, z \in E$, $(x \star y) \star (y \star y) = (z \star z) \star (x \star x)$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. For $x \in E$, the element $(x \star x) \star x$ is called the *trace* of $x$ and denoted $\tau(x)$. In what follows, $E$ denotes a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[Structure]]

### 2. Examples

On the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$, each of the following three laws is a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$: (A) $2 \star 2 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise; (B) $2 \star 1 = 2 \star 2 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise; (C) $1 \star 2 = 2 \star 1 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise. ## § 2. TRACES

PROPOSITION 1. — *For all $x, z, z' \in E$, $(z \star z) \star (x \star x) = (z' \star z') \star (x \star x)$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) with $y = x$, both sides are equal to $(x \star x) \star (x \star x)$. [[sqsq_left]]

PROPOSITION 2. — *For all $x, y \in E$, $(x \star y) \star (y \star y) = \tau(x)$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) with $z = x$, the left-hand side is equal to $(x \star x) \star (x \star x)$, which is $\tau(x)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[trace]]

PROPOSITION 3. — *For all $x, z \in E$, $(z \star z) \star (x \star x) = \tau(x)$.*

By Prop. 1, the left-hand side is equal to $(x \star x) \star (x \star x)$, which is $\tau(x)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[sqsq_trace]]

*Remark.* Thus the two sides of ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) are both equal to the trace of $x$; the axiom says nothing else.

## § 3. REMARKABLE ELEMENTS

### 1. Involutive elements

DEFINITION 1. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* involutive *if $(x \star a) \star (a \star x) = x$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d1]]

PROPOSITION 4. — *If $a$ is involutive, then $\tau(a) = a$, and $(z \star z) \star (a \star a) = a$ for every $z \in E$.*

Taking $x = a$ in Def. 1 gives $(a \star a) \star (a \star a) = a$; the left-hand side is $\tau(a)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I), and is equal to $(z \star z) \star (a \star a)$ by Prop. 1. [[d1_trace]] [[d1_sq]]

THEOREM. — *If $a$ is involutive, every element of $E$ equals $a$.* [[involutive_subsingleton]]

Write $s(x)=x\star x$ and $b=s(a)$. By axiom I and Prop. 4, $b\star b=a$ and $b\star a=a$. Prop. 3 gives $a\star b=a$, and Prop. 2 at $(a,b)$ now gives $b=a$. Thus Prop. 4 gives $s(z)\star a=a$, and Prop. 2 gives $	au(s(z))=a$. For any $u,v$, applying Prop. 2 twice yields $	au(u\star v)=	au(u)\star s(s(v))=	au(s(v))=a$, where the second equality follows from Prop. 3 and $	au(u)=s(s(u))$. Every element is a product by involutivity. Applying involutivity to $s(x)$ now gives $s(x)=a$, since $s(x)\star a=a$ and $a\star s(x)=	au(x)=a$. Hence $a\star x=s(x)\star x=	au(x)=a$, and involutivity at $x$ gives $x=(x\star a)\star a=	au(x)=a$.

### 2. Transparent, reciprocal and supported elements

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* transparent *if $x \star a = (a \star a) \star (a \star x)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d2]]

PROPOSITION 5. — *If $a$ is transparent, then $a \star a = \tau(a)$.*

Taking $x = a$ gives $a \star a = (a \star a) \star (a \star a)$, which is $\tau(a)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[d2_sq]]

DEFINITION 3. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* reciprocal *if $(a \star x) \star a = (x \star x) \star (x \star a)$ for every $x \in E$, and* supported *if there exists a reciprocal element $x$ such that $a \star x = a \star a$.* [[d3]] [[d4]]

PROPOSITION 6. — *Every reciprocal element is supported.*

Take $x = a$. [[d3_d4]]

PROPOSITION 7. — *A reciprocal element need not be transparent, and a supported element need not be reciprocal.*

In Example (A) of § 1, the element $2$ is reciprocal but not transparent; in Example (C), the element $2$ is supported but not reciprocal. [[exA_facts]] [[exC_d4_not_d3]]

### 3. Coherent Σ@S-sets

DEFINITION 4. — *A $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be* coherent *if $y \star (y \star y) = (x \star y) \star (y \star x)$ for all $x, y \in E$.* [[d5]]

PROPOSITION 8. — *The $\Sigma_{@N}$-set of Example (A) is coherent, and that of Example (B) is not.*

This is verified by inspection of the tables. [[exA_facts]] [[exB_not_d5]] Coherence is therefore neither a consequence of the axioms nor incompatible with them.
