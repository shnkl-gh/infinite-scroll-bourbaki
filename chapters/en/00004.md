# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is given by a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$, a mapping $\varphi$ of $E$ into itself and an element $e$ of $E$, subject to the following axioms:

($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) *For every* $x \in E$, $x \star x = \varphi(x)$.

($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) *For all* $x, y \in E$, *the relation* $y \star e = \varphi(y)$ *implies* $(y \star x) \star (y \star x) = (e \star y) \star (y \star y)$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*, and $e$ is called its *distinguished element*. In what follows, $E$ denotes a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[Structure]]

PROPOSITION 1. — *The mapping $\varphi$ is the mapping $x \mapsto x \star x$.*

This is axiom ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[phi_sq]] The mapping $\varphi$ is thus determined by the law $\star$, and the reader may replace $\varphi(x)$ by $x \star x$ wherever it occurs.

PROPOSITION 2. — *For every $x \in E$, $(e \star x) \star (e \star x) = (e \star e) \star (e \star e)$.*

Apply ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) with $y = e$; its hypothesis $e \star e = \varphi(e)$ holds by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[e_left_sq]]

### 2. Example

Let $\star$ be an idempotent and commutative law of composition on $E$, and $o$ an element of $E$ such that $y \star o = o$ for every $y \in E$; for instance, $E$ may be a lattice with a least element $o$, and $x \star y = \inf(x, y)$. Take for $\varphi$ the identity mapping and $e = o$. Then $E$ is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. Indeed, ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) expresses that $\star$ is idempotent; in ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), the hypothesis $y \star o = y$ means that $y = o$, and both sides are then equal to $o$. [[ofSemilatticeBot]]

## § 2. SOME CLASSES OF ELEMENTS

### 1. Regular, polar and dominant elements

DEFINITION 1. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* regular *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $\varphi(x) \star \varphi(a) = (x \star x) \star (a \star x)$.* [[d1]]

PROPOSITION 3. — *Every element of $E$ is regular.*

Take $x = a$, and use ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[d1_all]]

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* polar *if it is regular and $a \star e = \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d2]]

PROPOSITION 4. — *For an element $a$ of $E$ to be polar, it is necessary and sufficient that $x \star x = a \star e$ for every $x \in E$, that is, that all squares be equal to $a \star e$.*

This follows from Prop. 3 and Prop. 1. [[d2_iff]]

DEFINITION 3. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* dominant *if $\varphi(x) \star (a \star a) = (a \star e) \star (a \star a)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d5]]

PROPOSITION 5. — *Every polar element is dominant.*

If $a$ is polar, then $\varphi(x) = a \star e$ for every $x \in E$, and the relation of Def. 3 becomes an identity. [[d2_d5]]

### 2. Attracting and anchored elements

DEFINITION 4. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* attracting *if $x \star (x \star a) = \varphi(a \star a)$ for every $x \in E$, and* anchored *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $\varphi(x \star x) = a \star \varphi(a)$.* [[d3]] [[d4]]

PROPOSITION 6. — *Every attracting element is anchored.*

Let $a$ be attracting. Taking $x = a$ in Def. 4 gives $\varphi(a \star a) = a \star (a \star a)$, and $a \star a = \varphi(a)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I); hence $\varphi(a \star a) = a \star \varphi(a)$, and $x = a$ satisfies the condition for $a$ to be anchored. [[d3_d4]]

PROPOSITION 7. — *In the Example of § 1, an element $a$ is attracting if and only if $a = o$.*

If $a$ is attracting, taking $x = o$ gives $o \star (o \star a) = \varphi(a \star a)$; the left-hand side is $o$ and the right-hand side is $a$, so $a = o$. Conversely, $x \star (x \star o) = o = \varphi(o \star o)$ for every $x \in E$. [[bot_d3_iff]]
