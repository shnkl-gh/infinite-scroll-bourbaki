# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is given by a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ and a mapping $\varphi$ of $E$ into itself, subject to the following axioms:

($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) *For every* $x \in E$, $\varphi(x) \star (x \star x) = x$.

($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) *For all* $x, y, z \in E$, $(y \star y) \star (z \star x) = (z \star z) \star z$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. For $x \in E$, the element $x \star x$ is called the *square* of $x$, and an element of $E$ is called a *square* if it is the square of some element of $E$. In what follows, $E$ denotes a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[Structure]]

*Remark.* The right-hand side of axiom ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) depends neither on $x$ nor on $y$. The whole of this chapter rests on this observation.

### 2. Examples

1) Let $E$ be a set and $o$ an element of $E$. Put $x \star o = x$ and $x \star y = o$ for $y \neq o$, and let $\varphi$ be the identity mapping of $E$. Then $E$ is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. For $x \neq o$ we have $x \star x = o$, so that $\varphi(x) \star (x \star x) = x \star o = x$; the remaining verifications are left to the reader. [[pointed]]

2) The species $\Sigma_{@N}$ does not require $\varphi$ to be bijective. On the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$, the law and the mapping
$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} \star & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ \hline 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 & 1 \end{array} \qquad \varphi(0) = 0,\ \varphi(1) = 2,\ \varphi(2) = 2$$
define a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$. [[ex3]]

## § 2. SQUARES

### 1. Left translations by squares

PROPOSITION 1. — *Every element of $E$ is the product of two elements of $E$.*

Indeed, $x = \varphi(x) \star (x \star x)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I). [[op_surjective]]

PROPOSITION 2. — *For all $x, y, y' \in E$, we have $(y \star y) \star x = (y' \star y') \star x$.*

In other words, left multiplication by a square does not depend on the square.

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I), we may replace $x$ by $\varphi(x) \star (x \star x)$. Then ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), applied with $\varphi(x)$ in place of $z$ and $x \star x$ in place of $x$, gives $(y \star y) \star x = (\varphi(x) \star \varphi(x)) \star \varphi(x)$. The right-hand side does not depend on $y$, so the same computation with $y'$ in place of $y$ gives the same value. [[sq_left]]

PROPOSITION 3. — *For all $x, y, z \in E$, we have $(y \star y) \star (z \star x) = (y \star y) \star z$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), the left-hand side is $(z \star z) \star z$, and this equals $(y \star y) \star z$ by Prop. 2. [[sq_absorb]]

COROLLARY. — *For all $x, y \in E$, we have $(y \star y) \star \varphi(x) = (y \star y) \star x$.*

Write $x = \varphi(x) \star (x \star x)$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I), and apply Prop. 3 with $\varphi(x)$ in place of $z$. [[sq_phi]]

## § 3. SOME REMARKABLE ELEMENTS

### 1. Recurrent elements

DEFINITION 1. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* recurrent *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $x = (a \star x) \star x$.* [[d1]]

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is called a* recurrent square *if there exists a recurrent element $x$ such that $a = x \star x$.* [[d2]]

A recurrent square is evidently a square. [[d2_square]]

DEFINITION 3. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* $\varphi$-recurrent *if there exists a recurrent element $x$ such that $x = \varphi(a)$.* [[d3]]

PROPOSITION 4. — *For an element $a$ of $E$ to be $\varphi$-recurrent, it is necessary and sufficient that $\varphi(a)$ be recurrent.*

If $a$ is $\varphi$-recurrent, the recurrent element $x$ of Def. 3 is $\varphi(a)$. Conversely, if $\varphi(a)$ is recurrent, take $x = \varphi(a)$. [[d3_iff]]

In Example 1 of § 1, the element $o$ is recurrent, since $(o \star o) \star o = o$; it is therefore also a recurrent square and $\varphi$-recurrent. [[pointed_o_d1]] [[pointed_o_d2]] [[pointed_o_d3]]

### 2. Neutralising elements

DEFINITION 4. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* neutralising *if $\varphi(x) \star (x \star x) = (a \star a) \star \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d4]]

PROPOSITION 5. — *For an element $a$ of $E$ to be neutralising, it is necessary and sufficient that $(a \star a) \star x = x$ for every $x \in E$, that is, that the square of $a$ be a left identity element.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I), the left-hand side of the relation in Def. 4 is equal to $x$. By the Corollary of Prop. 3, the right-hand side is equal to $(a \star a) \star x$. [[d4_iff]]

PROPOSITION 6. — *If one element of $E$ is neutralising, then every element of $E$ is neutralising.*

This follows from Prop. 5 and Prop. 2. [[d4_all_or_none]]

☡ PROPOSITION 7. — *If $E$ possesses a neutralising element, then $E$ has at most one element.*

Let $a$ be a neutralising element. For all $z, w \in E$, Prop. 5 and Prop. 3 give
$$z \star w = (a \star a) \star (z \star w) = (a \star a) \star z = z.$$
Hence, for every $x \in E$, $x = (a \star a) \star x = a \star a = a$, so every element of $E$ is equal to $a$. [[d4_trivial]]

*Remark.* The notion of neutralising element, although correctly defined, is therefore of no interest except in the trivial case, and we shall not return to it.

### 3. Balanced Σ@S-sets

DEFINITION 5. — *A $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be* balanced *if $(y \star y) \star (x \star x) = (y \star x) \star (x \star x)$ for all $x, y \in E$.* [[d5]]

PROPOSITION 8. — *For $E$ to be balanced, it is necessary and sufficient that $(y \star x) \star (x \star x) = (y \star y) \star x$ for all $x, y \in E$.*

By Prop. 3, $(y \star y) \star (x \star x) = (y \star y) \star x$. [[d5_iff]]

The set of Example 1 of § 1 is not balanced when it has more than one element: for $y \neq o$ the left-hand side of Prop. 8, with $x = o$, is equal to $y$, while the right-hand side is equal to $o$. [[pointed_not_d5]]

PROPOSITION 9. — *A balanced $\Sigma_{@N}$-set has at most one element.* [[balanced_subsingleton]]

In a nonempty model, let $C(x)$ be left multiplication of $x$ by any square (Prop. 2). By Prop. 3, $C(C(t)) = C(z \star z) = C(z)$ for all $z,t$, so $C$ is constant, with value $k$. Write $s=x\star x$. Apply the balanced identity to $s$ and $arphi(x)$. Since $s\star s=k$ and $arphi(x)\star s=x$, this gives $x\star k=k$. Axiom I applied to $s$ then gives $s=arphi(s)\star k=k$. Applying it once more to $x$ gives $x=k$.
