# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ satisfying the following axioms:

($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) *For all* $x, y \in E$, $(y \star x) \star x = (y \star x) \star y$.

($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) *For all* $x, y, z \in E$, $(z \star y) \star (y \star z) = (x \star z) \star x$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. An element $\omega$ of a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be *left absorbing* if $\omega \star x = \omega$ for every $x \in E$. [[Structure]]

### 2. Examples

1) Let $\omega$ be an element of $E$ and $\star$ a law of composition on $E$ such that $(u \star v) \star w = \omega$ for all $u, v, w \in E$; for instance, the constant law $x \star y = \omega$. Each side of ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) and of ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) is a product of two elements multiplied on the right by a third, hence is equal to $\omega$; thus $E$ is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[nullClass2]]

2) On the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$, put $1 \star 1 = 2$, $2 \star 0 = 1$, and $x \star y = 0$ for all other pairs. This defines a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[exB]] It is not of the type of Example 1, for $(0 \star 0) \star 0 = 0$, whereas $(1 \star 1) \star 0 = 2 \star 0 = 1$.

## § 2. THE ABSORBING ELEMENT

### 1. Two lemmas

In this paragraph, $E$ denotes a non-empty $\Sigma_{@N}$-set, and we put $\kappa(z) = (z \star z) \star z$ for $z \in E$.

LEMMA 1. — *For all $x, y, z \in E$, we have*
$$(x \star z) \star x = \kappa(z), \tag{1}$$
$$(z \star y) \star (y \star z) = \kappa(z), \tag{2}$$
$$(y \star z) \star z = \kappa(z). \tag{3}$$

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), the element $(x \star z) \star x$ is equal to $(z \star z) \star (z \star z)$, whatever $x$ may be; taking $x = z$, we see that it is also equal to $(z \star z) \star z = \kappa(z)$, whence (1). [[L1]] Relation (2) is ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) with $x = z$. [[L2]] Finally, ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) gives $(y \star z) \star z = (y \star z) \star y$, and the right-hand side is equal to $\kappa(z)$ by (1). [[L3]]

LEMMA 2. — *For all $x, y \in E$, we have*
$$\kappa(y) \star x = \kappa(x). \tag{4}$$

By (1), $\kappa(y) = (x \star y) \star x$, so that $\kappa(y) \star x = ((x \star y) \star x) \star x$. Applying ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) with $x \star y$ in place of $y$, we obtain
$$((x \star y) \star x) \star x = ((x \star y) \star x) \star (x \star y),$$
and the right-hand side is equal to $\kappa(x)$ by (1), applied with $x \star y$ in place of $x$ and $x$ in place of $z$. [[L4]]

### 2. The absorbing element

THEOREM 1. — *Let $E$ be a non-empty $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. The mapping $\kappa$ is constant. If $\omega$ denotes its value:*

*(i) for all $x, y \in E$, $(x \star y) \star x = (x \star y) \star y = (x \star y) \star (y \star x) = \omega$;*

*(ii) $\omega$ is left absorbing;*

*(iii) $\omega$ is the only left absorbing element of $E$.*

Let $x, y \in E$. Relation (1), applied with $\kappa(y)$ in place of $x$ and $x$ in place of $z$, gives $(\kappa(y) \star x) \star \kappa(y) = \kappa(x)$. By (4), $\kappa(y) \star x = \kappa(x)$; and, again by (4), $\kappa(x) \star \kappa(y) = \kappa(\kappa(y))$. Hence
$$\kappa(x) = \kappa(\kappa(y)). \tag{5}$$
Taking $x = y$ in (5), we obtain $\kappa(y) = \kappa(\kappa(y))$; comparing with (5), we see that $\kappa(x) = \kappa(y)$, which proves that $\kappa$ is constant. [[L5]] [[kappa_const]]

Assertion (i) now follows from (1), (3) and (2), and assertion (ii) from (4). Finally, let $b$ be a left absorbing element of $E$. Then $b \star b = b$, hence $(b \star b) \star b = b$; but $(b \star b) \star b = \kappa(b) = \omega$, so that $b = \omega$. [[main]]

The element $\omega$ is called the *absorbing element* of $E$.

*Remark.* The absorbing element need not absorb on the right, and a product $(u \star v) \star w$ need not be equal to $\omega$. In Example 2 of § 1, the absorbing element is $0$, by Th. 1 (i); but $2 \star 0 = 1$, and $(1 \star 1) \star 0 = 1$. [[exB_facts]]

## § 3. APPLICATION TO SOME DEFINITIONS

In this paragraph, $E$ again denotes a non-empty $\Sigma_{@N}$-set, and $\omega$ its absorbing element.

Let $a$ be an element of $E$. We say that $a$ is *equilibrated* if there exists $x \in E$ such that $(x \star x) \star (a \star x) = (x \star a) \star (x \star x)$; that $a$ is *stabilised* if there exists an equilibrated element $x$ such that $x \star a = x$; and that $a$ is *quadratic* if there exists $x \in E$ such that $(x \star a) \star (x \star a) = (x \star a) \star a$. [[d1]] [[d2]] [[d4]]

PROPOSITION 1. — *Every element of $E$ is equilibrated, stabilised and quadratic.*

Let $a \in E$. Taking $x = a$, we see that $a$ is equilibrated; in particular, $\omega$ is equilibrated. Since $\omega \star a = \omega$ (Th. 1 (ii)), the element $x = \omega$ shows that $a$ is stabilised. Finally, with $x = \omega$ we have $(\omega \star a) \star (\omega \star a) = \omega \star \omega = \omega$ and $(\omega \star a) \star a = \omega \star a = \omega$, so that $a$ is quadratic. [[d1_all]] [[d2_all]] [[d4_all]]

These three notions are therefore of no interest in a non-empty $\Sigma_{@N}$-set.

A $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be *rigid* if $x = (x \star x) \star (y \star y)$ for all $x, y \in E$, and *involutive* if $(x \star y) \star (y \star x) = x$ for all $x, y \in E$. [[d3]] [[d5]]

☡ PROPOSITION 2. — *A rigid or involutive $\Sigma_{@N}$-set has at most one element.*

In either case, taking $y = x$ gives $x = (x \star x) \star (x \star x)$ for every $x \in E$, and the right-hand side is equal to $\kappa(x)$ by (2). Since $\kappa$ is constant (Th. 1), all elements of $E$ are equal. [[d3_trivial]] [[d5_trivial]]
