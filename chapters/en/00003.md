# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is given by a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ and a mapping $\varphi$ of $E$ into itself, subject to the following axiom:

($\Sigma_{@N}$) *For all* $x, y \in E$, $x \star (x \star x) = \varphi(y)$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. For $x \in E$, the element $x \star (x \star x)$ is called the *cube* of $x$. In what follows, $E$ denotes a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[Structure]]

### 2. The apex

PROPOSITION 1. — *For every $x \in E$, $\varphi(x)$ is the cube of $x$.*

Take $y = x$ in ($\Sigma_{@N}$). [[phi_eq_cube]]

PROPOSITION 2. — *The mapping $\varphi$ is constant, and all elements of $E$ have the same cube.*

For $y, y' \in E$, axiom ($\Sigma_{@N}$) gives $\varphi(y) = y \star (y \star y) = \varphi(y')$. For $x, x' \in E$, it gives $x \star (x \star x) = \varphi(x) = x' \star (x' \star x')$. [[phi_const]] [[cube_const]]

When $E$ is not empty, the common value of the cubes is called the *apex* of $E$ and denoted $c$; thus $\varphi(y) = c$ for every $y \in E$.

COROLLARY. — *The apex is its own cube, and $\varphi \circ \varphi = \varphi$.*

Indeed, $c \star (c \star c) = \varphi(c) = c$ by ($\Sigma_{@N}$), and $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi(y)$ by Prop. 2. [[apex_cube]] [[phi_idem]]

*Remark.* Conversely, let $\star$ be a law of composition on $E$ such that all cubes are equal to one element $c$, and let $\varphi$ be the constant mapping with value $c$; then $\star$ and $\varphi$ define a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$. [[ofConstCube]] By Prop. 2, every structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ on a non-empty set arises in this way: the mapping $\varphi$ adds nothing to the law $\star$.

## § 2. STABLE AND QUADRATIC ELEMENTS

### 1. Definitions

DEFINITION 1. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* left stable *if $a = \varphi(x) \star (x \star a)$ for every $x \in E$, and* right stable *if $a = (x \star a) \star \varphi(a)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d1]] [[d3]]

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* quadratic *if $(a \star a) \star (x \star x) = (a \star x) \star x$ for every $x \in E$, and* conservative *if $a \star x$ is quadratic for every quadratic element $x$.* [[d2]] [[d4]]

### 2. Independence of these notions

PROPOSITION 3. — *None of the properties "left stable" and "right stable" implies the other, and none of the properties "quadratic" and "conservative" implies the other.*

On the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$, with $\varphi$ constant with value $0$, consider the two laws
$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} \star & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ \hline 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \qquad\qquad \begin{array}{c|ccc} \star & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ \hline 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array}$$
Each of them defines a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$. For the first, the element $0$ is left stable but not right stable; for the second, it is right stable but not left stable. [[exP_d1_not_d3]] [[exQ_d3_not_d1]]

On the set $\{0, 1\}$, with $\varphi$ constant with value $0$, take first $0 \star 0 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise: the element $0$ is quadratic but not conservative. Take next $0 \star 1 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise: the element $1$ is conservative but not quadratic. [[exR_d2_not_d4]] [[exS_d4_not_d2]]

*Remark.* We know nothing more about these elements.
