# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is given by a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ and a relation $\rho$ between elements of $E$, subject to the following axiom:

($\Sigma_{@N}$) *For all* $x, y \in E$, $(x \star x) \star (x \star y) = (y \star y) \star (x \star x)$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. [[Structure]]

*Remark.* The relation $\rho$ does not appear in axiom ($\Sigma_{@N}$). If $E$ is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set, one obtains another structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ by keeping the law $\star$ and replacing $\rho$ by an arbitrary relation. [[withRel]] The relation $\rho$ is thus an ornament, which the axiom neither constrains nor uses.

### 2. Examples

1) Let $\star$ be a law of composition on $E$ which is idempotent, commutative and associative; for instance, $E$ may be a lattice and $x \star y = \inf(x, y)$. Then $E$, endowed with $\star$ and with any relation $\rho$, is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set: both sides of ($\Sigma_{@N}$) are equal to $x \star y$. [[ofSemilattice]]

2) Let $o$ be an element of $E$ and $\star$ a law on $E$ such that $x \star x = o$ and $o \star z = o$ for all $x, z \in E$. Then both sides of ($\Sigma_{@N}$) are equal to $o$, and $E$ is a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set for any relation $\rho$. [[absorbing]] For instance, on the set $\{0, 1\}$ one may take $1 \star 0 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ otherwise. [[exA]]

## § 2. ELEMENTS AND LAWS

### 1. Admissible and conformable elements

DEFINITION 1. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* admissible *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $(x \star a) \star (x \star x) = (x \star x) \star (x \star x)$.* [[d2]]

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* conformable *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $(x \star x) \star (x \star x) = (x \star a) \star (x \star a)$.* [[d3]]

PROPOSITION 1. — *Every element of $E$ is admissible and conformable.*

It suffices to take $x = a$ in Def. 1 and Def. 2. [[d2_all]] [[d3_all]]

*Remark.* The notions of admissible and conformable element are therefore of no interest; they have been introduced only to be dismissed.

### 2. Subordinate elements

DEFINITION 3. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* subordinate *if $(a \star x) \mathrel{\rho} (x \star x)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d1]]

PROPOSITION 2. — *Let $\star$ be an idempotent, commutative and associative law on $E$, and let $\rho$ be the relation $u \star v = u$ (that is, $u \leq v$ for the order associated with $\star$). Then every element of $E$ is subordinate.*

For $a, x \in E$, we have $(a \star x) \star (x \star x) = (a \star x) \star x = a \star (x \star x) = a \star x$. [[ofSemilattice_d1]]

### 3. Absorptive laws

DEFINITION 4. — *The law of a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be* absorptive *if $(x \star y) \star x = x \star y$ for all $x, y \in E$.* [[d4]]

PROPOSITION 3. — *An idempotent, commutative and associative law is absorptive.*

Indeed, $(x \star y) \star x = x \star (x \star y) = (x \star x) \star y = x \star y$. [[ofSemilattice_d4]]

PROPOSITION 4. — *The law of Example 2 of § 1 on $\{0, 1\}$ is not absorptive.*

Indeed, $(1 \star 0) \star 1 = 1 \star 1 = 0$, whereas $1 \star 0 = 1$. [[exA_not_d4]]

Axiom ($\Sigma_{@N}$) therefore does not imply that the law is absorptive.
