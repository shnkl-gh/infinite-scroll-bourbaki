# CHAPTER @N. STRUCTURES OF SPECIES Σ@S

## § 1. THE SPECIES Σ@S

### 1. Definition

Let $E$ be a set. A *structure of species* $\Sigma_{@N}$ on $E$ is given by a law of composition $(x, y) \mapsto x \star y$ on $E$ and a mapping $\varphi$ of $E$ into itself, subject to the following axioms:

($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) *For all* $x, y \in E$, $\varphi(x \star x) = (x \star y) \star y$.

($\Sigma_{@N}$ II) *For all* $x, y \in E$, $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi(x \star y)$.

A set endowed with a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$ is called a $\Sigma_{@N}$*-set*. In what follows, $E$ denotes a $\Sigma_{@N}$-set. [[Structure]]

### 2. Example

On the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$, put $2 \star 2 = 1$ and $x \star y = 0$ for all other pairs, and let $\varphi(0) = \varphi(1) = 0$ and $\varphi(2) = 1$. This defines a structure of species $\Sigma_{@N}$. [[exA]]

## § 2. THE RESIDUE

### 1. The mapping φ ∘ φ

LEMMA 1. — *For every $x \in E$, $\varphi(\varphi(x)) = \varphi(x \star x)$.*

Take $y = x$ in ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II). [[phiphi_sq]]

PROPOSITION 1. — *For all $x, y \in E$, $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi(\varphi(\varphi(x)))$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), applied to $x \star y$ and $y$, we have $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi((x \star y) \star y)$. By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I), $(x \star y) \star y = \varphi(x \star x)$, and by Lemma 1, $\varphi(x \star x) = \varphi(\varphi(x))$. Hence $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi(\varphi(\varphi(x)))$. [[key]]

PROPOSITION 2. — *The mapping $\varphi \circ \varphi$ is constant.*

For $x, y \in E$, Prop. 1 gives $\varphi(\varphi(x)) = \varphi(\varphi(\varphi(y)))$, and, applied with $x = y$, $\varphi(\varphi(y)) = \varphi(\varphi(\varphi(y)))$. Hence $\varphi(\varphi(x)) = \varphi(\varphi(y))$. [[phiphi_const]]

When $E$ is not empty, the common value of $\varphi \circ \varphi$ is called the *residue* of $E$ and denoted $k$.

### 2. Consequences

PROPOSITION 3. — *For all $x, y \in E$, $(x \star y) \star y = k$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ I) and Lemma 1, $(x \star y) \star y = \varphi(x \star x) = \varphi(\varphi(x)) = k$. [[rsq]] [[rsq_const]]

PROPOSITION 4. — *For all $x, z \in E$, $\varphi(z \star x) = k$.*

By ($\Sigma_{@N}$ II), $\varphi(z \star x) = \varphi(\varphi(x)) = k$. [[phi_prod]]

*Remark.* Nothing in the axioms suggests that the products $(x \star y) \star y$ should all coincide; it is the composite $\varphi \circ \varphi$ which forces it. In the Example of § 1, the residue is $0$. [[exA_residue]]

## § 3. SOME DEFINITIONS

DEFINITION 1. — *A $\Sigma_{@N}$-set $E$ is said to be* tame *if $\varphi(\varphi(x)) = (y \star y) \star y$ for all $x, y \in E$.* [[d1]]

PROPOSITION 5. — *Every $\Sigma_{@N}$-set is tame.*

Both sides are equal to $k$, by Prop. 2 and Prop. 3. [[d1_holds]]

DEFINITION 2. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* annihilating *if $(x \star x) \star x = a \star (x \star x)$ for every $x \in E$.* [[d3]]

PROPOSITION 6. — *For an element $a$ of $E$ to be annihilating, it is necessary and sufficient that $a \star (x \star x) = k$ for every $x \in E$.*

Indeed, $(x \star x) \star x = k$ by Prop. 3. [[d3_iff]]

DEFINITION 3. — *An element $a$ of $E$ is said to be* reflected *if there exists $x \in E$ such that $a = \varphi(x) \star (x \star a)$; one then says that $a$ is reflected by $x$.* [[d2]]

PROPOSITION 7. — *If $a$ is reflected by $x$, then $a \star (x \star a) = k$.*

By Prop. 3, applied to $\varphi(x)$ and $x \star a$, we have $(\varphi(x) \star (x \star a)) \star (x \star a) = k$; and $\varphi(x) \star (x \star a) = a$. [[d2_witness]]
